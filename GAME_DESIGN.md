# Game design

## Design thesis

AptiBloom is a learning system with a gentle game world, not a game interrupted by questions. Every mechanic must cause a useful learning behavior: attempting, explaining, correcting, revisiting, or applying under pressure.

## Theme: the skill garden

The home screen is a calm garden/map divided into quantitative, logical, and verbal regions. Concepts begin as seeds, develop as understanding becomes stable, and bloom after spaced review. “Placement Ready” adds a distinct marker only after timed performance is demonstrated.

The garden gives an emotional sense of growth without pretending that decorative progress equals mastery.

## Progress layers

| Layer | Meaning | Earned through |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Concept mastery | Actual skill estimate | Accurate, varied, spaced performance |
| Placement readiness | Speed plus accuracy | Timed mixed and simulated tests |
| Journey level | Overall product progression | Completing meaningful learning activities |
| Petals | Cosmetic currency | First-attempt work, retries, reviews, helping a squad |
| Collection | Optional visual personalization | Spending petals on themes, plants, and avatar items |

Petals never buy answers, easier tests, or mastery.

## Core modes

### Daily Bloom

A 5–8 minute recommended set mixing one current skill, one weak skill, and one due review. This is the default home action.

### Concept Lab

A short interactive explanation followed by guided practice. No countdown. Students manipulate or complete steps rather than only reading.

### Focus Run

Untimed practice in one chosen topic. Difficulty adapts gradually and the student may request a hint.

### Recovery Mission

Triggered by a recurring misconception. It contains one micro-explanation, two near-neighbour questions, and one transfer question. The tone is “repair this skill,” not punishment.

### Topic Trial

A 10–15 question mixed checkpoint that hides subtopic labels. Passing shows durable concept mastery; it does not yet certify speed.

### Placement Sprint

A short timed set unlocked after topic mastery. It trains decisions about skipping, estimating, and allocating time.

### Placement Simulation

A realistic mixed or company-pattern test with timer, navigation, marking for review, sectional rules, and detailed post-test analysis. No playful animations occur during the simulation.

### Squad Garden

Three to six friends contribute mastery actions to a weekly cooperative goal. Examples include “complete 30 due reviews” or “help the squad repair five weak concepts.” The squad can compare improvement, but raw rank and lowest performer are hidden by default.

## Session rhythm

1. Calm arrival and one recommended action.
2. Clear mission goal and expected duration.
3. Low-stakes attempt.
4. Short interaction/explanation.
5. Practice with fading support.
6. One spaced review.
7. Mastery update and small visual reward.
8. A deliberate stop point; continuing is optional.

The app should not use endless auto-play to keep a student trapped.

## Rewards

Reward:

- first-attempt reasoning;
- completing a retry after an error;
- returning for a scheduled review;
- improving a personal best;
- using an explanation then solving independently;
- helping a squad reach a learning goal; and
- completing a balanced weekly plan.

Do not reward:

- random tapping;
- time spent while idle;
- maintaining an unhealthy session length;
- inviting contacts without consent;
- repeating memorized easy questions for currency; or
- beating a much weaker learner.

## Streak design

Use a flexible weekly “rhythm” of three or four learning days rather than a fragile daily streak. Missing a day does not erase visible progress. Optional reminders say what is due and how long it will take; they do not use guilt.

## Competition

- Off by default during concept learning.
- Optional friend-only or ability-banded challenges.
- Accuracy ranking precedes speed ranking.
- Team-versus-team events combine competition with collaboration.
- Public global leaderboards are excluded from the MVP.
- Students can hide all social comparison without losing features.

## Failure experience

A wrong answer:

1. pauses game celebration;
2. shows the likely mistake without ridicule;
3. offers the smallest useful hint;
4. reveals a worked solution progressively;
5. provides a similar retry; and
6. schedules another retrieval later.

The learner does not lose lives or access to the topic.

## Anti-patterns

- A slot-machine reward animation after every question.
- Confetti that interrupts problem solving.
- Timer pressure while learning a new method.
- XP standing in for skill evidence.
- Multiple confusing currencies.
- “You lost your streak” guilt messages.
- Fake urgency or pay-to-repair progress.
- Cosmetic narrative so heavy that it obscures placement preparation.
