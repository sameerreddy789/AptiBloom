# Requirements extracted from the conversation

## Explicit needs

The discussion establishes the following requirements:

- The app teaches aptitude for placement preparation.
- Current aptitude training feels boring and difficult to understand.
- The experience should feel like a game so students remain interested.
- Kahoot was tried and felt too basic for this purpose.
- Questions must be real aptitude questions, not general trivia.
- Mathematics topics include percentages, ages, ratios, and related word problems.
- English topics include verbal ability and grammar.
- More topics may be added after research.
- Topics need planned question counts.
- Questions need difficulty levels.
- The product needs tests as well as learning/practice.
- The aesthetic should feel contemporary and appealing to Gen Z.
- Learning should feel calm rather than like another stressful class.
- The work should be substantial enough to help the student developers learn and strengthen their résumés.
- One collaborator will help develop the product while learning how the implementation works.

## Implied requirements

- The first target is likely Indian college students preparing for campus placement filters.
- The app needs quantitative aptitude, logical reasoning, and verbal ability even though only maths and English were initially named.
- Mobile access matters because students will practise around classes.
- Explanations are more important than simply marking an answer wrong.
- Both untimed learning and realistic timed assessment are necessary.
- Content quality is a core product function, not seed data to add at the end.
- Development should be teachable and modular so two early-career contributors can own clear features.

## Product interpretation

The best interpretation is **a placement-preparation learning game**, not:

- a teacher-hosted quiz creator like Kahoot;
- a collection of unrelated mini-games;
- a static aptitude-question website with badges;
- an endless speed test; or
- a general brain-training app disconnected from placement patterns.

## Working decisions

- Working title: AptiBloom.
- Platform: responsive web app/PWA first.
- Audience: college students preparing for campus placements.
- Core areas: quantitative, logical, and verbal aptitude.
- Session length: 5–8 minutes for daily learning; longer tests separately.
- Primary mode: self-paced mastery.
- Social mode: optional friend squads and cooperative challenges.
- Visual tone: calm, modern, soft, uncluttered, and mature enough for college students.
- Content launch: three-topic prototype, then twelve-topic MVP.
- AI-generated questions: not published automatically; every question requires human review.

## Product principle derived from the conversation

“Interesting” and “calm” must both survive. The app should create curiosity, feedback, and progress without noisy animations, constant countdowns, or public comparison. Timed pressure belongs in placement simulation, not in the first encounter with a concept.
