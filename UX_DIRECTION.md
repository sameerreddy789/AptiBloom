# UX and visual direction

## Desired feeling

**Calm enough to think. Alive enough to return. Serious enough to trust for placements.**

“Gen Z aesthetic” is interpreted as contemporary, expressive, customizable, and mobile-native—not as excessive slang, neon clutter, or constant memes.

## Visual principles

- Generous spacing and one primary action per screen.
- Soft neutral backgrounds with restrained botanical/aurora gradients.
- Deep readable text, not low-contrast pastel text.
- Rounded cards and organic shapes used consistently.
- One expressive display face paired with a highly readable body typeface.
- Dark and light themes from the start.
- Small purposeful motion showing cause, progress, or transformation.
- Illustrations and garden elements around the learning area, never behind problem text.

## Suggested palette direction

- Background: warm off-white and deep charcoal.
- Primary: muted indigo or blue-violet.
- Growth accent: sage/emerald.
- Warm accent: peach/coral used sparingly.
- Correct: accessible green plus an icon/label.
- Incorrect: accessible red/orange plus explanation; never colour alone.
- Focus: high-contrast blue outline.

Exact tokens should be tested for WCAG contrast before implementation.

## Screen map

### Welcome and diagnostic

- Plain explanation of the placement goal.
- Choice: diagnostic, choose a topic, or preview as guest.
- Estimated time visible before starting.

### Home / skill garden

- Today’s recommended 5–8 minute mission.
- Three domain regions with visible mastery/readiness.
- Due reviews and placement date.
- Quiet squad update below the personal plan.

### Topic path

- Prerequisites and concept nodes.
- Learn, practise, review, and trial states.
- Clear reason for locks and how to unlock them.
- No artificial waiting timer.

### Learning screen

- Problem or visual occupies the centre.
- Scratch space/calculator policy is explicit.
- Hint and “I’m not sure” are easy to find.
- Progress indicator shows steps, not urgency.
- Timer absent unless the learner chose a timed mode.

### Feedback screen

- Immediate result.
- Specific misconception or successful strategy.
- Progressive explanation with one focused step at a time.
- Retry action is primary after an error.
- Report-question action remains accessible.

### Progress

- Mastery and placement readiness displayed separately.
- Accuracy trend, due reviews, weak concepts, and time trend.
- Personal improvement before social comparison.
- Plain-language explanation of every score.

### Simulation

- Visually neutral exam interface.
- Persistent timer, navigation grid, mark for review, and section rules.
- No garden animations or reward interruptions until submission.

## Interaction types

- Multiple choice.
- Numeric answer.
- Drag steps into order.
- Match ratios/relationships.
- Highlight the grammatical error.
- Rearrange sentences.
- Select relevant information from a word problem.
- Complete the next line of a solution.
- Manipulate a small visual model.

Variety must reflect the thinking required, not novelty for its own sake.

## Motion and sound

- Motion duration is short and can be disabled.
- Growth animation occurs at session end, not after every answer.
- Correctness is understandable with sound off.
- Sound effects are subtle and muted by default during study.
- Reduced-motion mode replaces movement with fades or static state changes.

## Writing tone

- Short, respectful, and encouraging.
- Indian placement context without forced slang.
- “Let’s repair this step” instead of “Wrong!”
- “Your accuracy is ready; now train speed” instead of “Too slow.”
- “Three reviews are due—about six minutes” instead of “Don’t break your streak.”

## Accessibility checklist

- Full keyboard path.
- Logical heading and landmark structure.
- Visible focus states.
- Screen-reader labels for equations and diagrams.
- Alternative text for non-verbal reasoning items.
- Text zoom without clipped controls.
- No essential drag-only interaction; provide selection alternatives.
- Colour-blind-safe status design.
- Reduced motion and quiet mode.
