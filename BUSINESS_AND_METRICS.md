# Business model and success metrics

## Initial objective

The first objective is to prove learning value and build a strong student portfolio project. Monetization should not distort the prototype.

## North-star measure

**Weekly skills newly mastered and still recalled after a later review.**

This is better than raw time, questions answered, or XP because it combines product use with durable learning evidence.

## Learning metrics

- Diagnostic-to-post-test improvement.
- Seven-day and thirty-day retention.
- First-attempt accuracy by skill.
- Success after misconception repair.
- Transfer performance in mixed questions.
- Mastery-to-simulation validity.
- Accuracy before and after speed training.
- Hint dependence over time.

## Product metrics

- Activation: completes first mission and feedback loop.
- Mission completion rate.
- Percentage completing a second session within seven days.
- Weekly active learners.
- Due-review completion.
- Four-week retention.
- Voluntary continuation after a mission.
- Topic and test completion.
- Explanation helpfulness.

## Guardrail metrics

- Question dispute rate and confirmed-error rate.
- Reported anxiety or pressure.
- Session abandonment after wrong answers.
- Excessively long sessions.
- Notification opt-out and complaint rate.
- Performance gaps across device quality and accessibility needs.
- Social comparison opt-out and negative feedback.

Do not optimize engagement while these guardrails worsen.

## Freemium hypothesis after validation

| Plan | Possible price | Scope |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Free | ₹0 | Daily missions, prototype/core topics, basic progress, limited simulations |
| Plus | ₹149/month or ₹999/year | Full topics, advanced reports, company patterns, unlimited simulations, cosmetics |
| Campus | Roughly ₹100–₹300 per student per semester at volume | Cohort setup, facilitator reports, assignments, support, and institutional controls |

Pricing must be tested with real students and colleges. Do not paywall explanations to mistakes; that would undermine the learning promise.

## Ethical monetization

- No advertising inside active problem solving.
- No paid mastery, answer reveal, or easier scoring.
- No loot boxes or chance-based purchases.
- Cosmetic purchases, if any, show exact contents.
- Free users retain access to core learning and their own data.
- Student data is not sold to recruiters without a separate explicit consent model.

## Validation experiments

1. Compare the BLOOM loop against a plain question/solution flow on delayed performance.
2. Test skill garden versus a neutral progress dashboard without changing instructional content.
3. Compare flexible weekly rhythm with daily streak messaging.
4. Compare cooperative squad goals with individual competition.
5. Test whether company-pattern readiness increases willingness to pay.
6. Ask for a refundable Plus pre-order only after the MVP demonstrates value.

## Business risks

- High cost of authoring/reviewing questions and solutions.
- Content errors can destroy trust quickly.
- Students may use the app intensely only near placements.
- Strong free incumbents limit consumer pricing.
- Company patterns change and can be inaccurately represented.
- Adaptive claims invite scrutiny if readiness scores are not validated.
- A calm experience can be mistaken for low energy unless interaction design remains satisfying.

## Defensibility

The moat is not the first set of questions. It can grow from:

- a structured misconception graph;
- validated learning and readiness data;
- high-quality interactive explanations;
- calibrated placement item statistics;
- a trusted content-review process;
- college/company pattern mappings; and
- a recognizable calm social learning experience.
